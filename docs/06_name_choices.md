# How the name was chosen

**Chosen: Maṇichchilambu (மணிச்சிலம்பு), "the gem anklet".** In plain ASCII it's `manichchilambu`. The strict scholarly transliteration (ISO 15919) is *maṇiccilampu*. We use the spelling people say aloud, the same way the site writes Silappatikāram rather than Cilappatikāram.

**Why this name.** The case at the centre of Silappatikāram is settled by what's inside an anklet: the queen's has pearls, and Kannagi's has gems. Kannagi says hers holds மணி, gems (மணியுடை அரியே, SIL-20-0078), and she breaks it open in court (அணிமணிக் காற்சிலம்பு, SIL-20-0082). மணி also opens the name of the second epic, Maṇimēkalai. So the one word holds both poems, and the test the whole project is built on: look at what's actually there, not at what something looks like.

The exact compound மணிச்சிலம்பு isn't a word in either poem. It's built from those two lines, and a Tamil scholar should confirm it reads well before it goes on a paper.

The method is the **Maṇichchilambu ledger**, with the **Maṇichchilambu echo finder** and **Maṇichchilambu formula finder** alongside it.

## The other options considered at the start

| # | Name | Tamil | Meaning, and why it fits | Where it's from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tērvu** | தேர்வு | Examination. Kannagi's first words to the king are "தேரா மன்னா", king who did not examine. The whole method is about not doing what he did | SIL-20-0061, SIL-16-0149 |
| 2 | **Kural** | குரல் | Voice. Every fact in the graph carries whose voice it comes through. The catch: it sounds like Thirukkural, people may mix them up | SIL-30-0164 |
| 3 | **Vaḻakkurai** | வழக்குரை | Pleading a case with evidence, the title of canto 20 | SIL canto 20 |
| 4 | **Kaṭṭurai** | கட்டுரை | A statement that holds firm. Madurapati calls herself கட்டுரை யாட்டி, and she's the one who gives the deep explanation | SIL-23-0023 |
| 5 | **Uḷvari** | உள்வரி | A disguise or assumed role, from dance. Fits appearance vs reality, though it undersells the rest | MAN-21-0010, SIL-08-0089 |
| 6 | **Ūḻ** | ஊழ் | The working-out of past deeds. Suits the chains of cause and the echoes, but it pulls the name towards fate alone | SIL-16-0217, MAN-20-0123 |
| 7 | **Surabhi** | சுரபி | From அமுதசுரபி, the bowl that never runs out, one object that passes through many hands. Easy to say, a little sweet for a research method | MAN-11-0044 |
| 8 | **Iraṭṭai** | இரட்டை | "Twin", the usual Tamil name for this pair of epics. Clear, but says nothing about the method | Scholarly usage, it isn't a word from these lines |
| 9 | **Meyyuru** | மெய் + உரு | Truth + form: what's real next to what it looks like. Coined by me, so have someone check that the compound sits right | Coined |

I dropped பரல், the first word I thought of for the anklet's beads. In these texts it means pebbles on a hard road (SIL-10-0052, SIL-13-0004), so the meaning wouldn't hold.
